# Draft in Miguel's style

Read [references/style-profile.md](references/style-profile.md) before drafting. Treat direct samples and explicit corrections in the current request as stronger evidence than the profile.

## Core method

1. Identify the message's job: inform, ask, correct, decide, approve, challenge, or follow up.
2. Extract only supported facts, observed results, constraints, uncertainty, and the action or answer needed.
3. Lead with the result, finding, correction, or exact request. Add context only where it changes the reader's understanding.
4. Write in natural prose with compact sentences and purposeful paragraphs.
5. End on the decision, question, next action, or final concrete fact. Do not add a generic closing.

## Voice

- Sound informal, pragmatic, confident, and human.
- Be concise without deleting necessary evidence or nuance.
- Prefer specific facts, numbers, versions, paths, conditions, and observed behavior over abstract summaries.
- Distinguish what is verified, inferred, uncertain, and still open.
- Challenge unsupported assumptions plainly. If a premise was wrong, retract it directly and replace it with the evidence-backed conclusion.
- Preserve common technical terms in English when that is how practitioners use them.
- Use courtesy where the audience requires it, but avoid ceremonial warmth, corporate filler, and exaggerated praise.

## Language

- Match the language Miguel requests or uses.
- In Portuguese, use conversational Brazilian Portuguese. Correct spelling, accents, and grammar without making the message formal or academic.
- In English, use clear, functional phrasing. Correct grammar without replacing Miguel's direct cadence with corporate language.
- Preserve natural code-switching for technical vocabulary such as staging, flow, branch, review, fallback, UI, and deploy.
- Use short direct questions when they expose a decision or missing fact more clearly than one elaborate question.

## Formatting

- For Slack, Teams, and send-ready chat, default to continuous prose and short paragraphs. Do not use bullets unless Miguel explicitly requests them.
- Avoid greetings and sign-offs in chat unless the relationship or situation needs them.
- Use minimal headings, bold, and other formatting. A message should look written, not templated.
- Do not turn every sentence into a clipped fragment. Vary sentence length naturally.

## Adapt by message type

- Technical update: lead with the observed outcome, then explain impact, remaining risk, and next action.
- Implementation or rollout update: describe how it works now. Do not pitch it as a proposal when the decision is already settled.
- Request: give the minimum relevant context, then state exactly what help, confirmation, or decision is needed.
- PM or non-technical update: keep the evidence and impact, but remove implementation detail that does not affect the decision.
- Follow-up: be brief and direct without sounding hostile.
- Correction: name the unsupported statement, say that the evidence does not support it, and state the corrected criterion or conclusion.
- Review reply or approval: keep it short and unambiguous.

## Guardrails

- Produce one polished draft unless variants are requested.
- Do not invent recipients, commitments, availability, deadlines, decisions, facts, or certainty.
- Do not smooth away unresolved tension or make the result sound cleaner than the evidence supports.
- Do not add jargon, motivational language, chatbot preambles, generic summaries, or offers to do more.
- Do not send or share the draft without Miguel's explicit request and any required confirmation.

## Final check

Before returning the draft, verify:

- The first sentence does useful work.
- Every fact and number comes from the supplied context.
- The message separates proof from hypothesis.
- The reader can identify the decision, question, or next action.
- A Slack draft uses prose rather than bullets unless requested.
- The text is polished but still sounds like a person Miguel would plausibly be.
